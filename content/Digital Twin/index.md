	- We propose a three-step procedure that corresponds to the chronological order of the activities required to implement a DT. The first step explains how to design a DT, i.e., how to define the functional requirements and the system architecture. The second step explains how to model a DT. This step requires creating system models that represent different aspects of the PO and integrating them. Finally, the third step explains how to implement a DT, i.e., which are the existing platforms, frameworks and tools for developing a DT, how to synchronize the data between physical and virtual objects and the existing communication protocols and standards for the information exchange.
	- The DT concept was fist introduced in 2002 by Michael Grieves, and since then, the concepts and its associated technologies have evolved significantly. A detailed review of the DT history can be found in [[6](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/22/14/5396#B6-sensors-22-05396),[7](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/22/14/5396#B7-sensors-22-05396)].
	- A DT is composed of a real space containing a physical object, a virtual space containing a virtual object and a link for data flow from the real space to the virtual space and information flow from the virtual space to the real space [[8](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/22/14/5396#B8-sensors-22-05396)]. Hence, a DT can be seen as a digital copy of a physical object (PO) and its process [[9](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/22/14/5396#B9-sensors-22-05396)]. The PO’s behavior is converted into a virtual object (VO); both components are interconnected with a high level of synchronization between them [[10](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/22/14/5396#B10-sensors-22-05396),[11](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/22/14/5396#B11-sensors-22-05396)]. This link enables data exchange allowing physical–digital convergence [[12](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/22/14/5396#B12-sensors-22-05396)]. DTs are normally used in Cyber–Physical Systems (CPS) and networks as they integrate simulation of a physical product. For that, DT models the mechanical, electrical, software and other properties to optimize the physical product based on the updated real-time data synchronized from sensors [[13](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/22/14/5396#B13-sensors-22-05396)].
